## The Popcorn is Hot (Among Other Things)

Welcome to *Siskel & Ebert Present\**, where we review the quirkiest, boldest, and most underrepresented stories hitting the screen. Today, we’re diving into a topic often ignored in Hollywood: **menopause**. Yes, filmmakers are finally starting to tackle this universal experience, blending humor, drama, and heart in a variety of films. So, grab your fans and herbal tea, and let’s break down menopause on the silver screen.

### **1. The (M) Factor: Shredding the Silence on Menopause**

- **Ebert:** *"This documentary is a triumph! It exposes the healthcare system's failure to address menopause while giving women a voice. The expert interviews are enlightening, and the personal stories are powerful. A must-watch for anyone wanting to understand the long-overlooked impacts of menopause."*
- **Siskel:** *"I couldn’t agree more, Roger. It’s as educational as it is empowering. The call for systemic change makes this film more than just a documentary—it’s a movement. Two big thumbs up!"*

## ‍**2. Menopause the Musical** (Filmed Stage Production)

- **Siskel:** *"This one’s a riot! Four women harmonizing about hot flashes and mood swings in a department store? It’s relatable and ridiculously fun. The music is catchy, and the humor is pitch-perfect."*
- **Ebert:** *"I had my doubts going in, but you’re right—it’s delightful. It’s rare for a musical to be this on-the-nose about such a taboo topic, and it works. A thumbs up from me too!"*

### **3. The Hot Flashes** (2013)

- **Ebert:** *"While the story of middle-aged women forming a basketball team for charity is heartwarming, the film doesn’t dig too deeply into menopause itself. It’s more of a feel-good comedy, but Brooke Shields and Wanda Sykes bring charm and energy."*
- **Siskel:** *"True, but it’s refreshing to see women over 40 as the heroes of a sports comedy. Not every movie needs to be groundbreaking—sometimes fun is enough. One thumbs up!"*

### **4. Sex and the City 2** (2010)

- **Siskel:** *"Let’s be honest, this film’s plot was all over the place. But Samantha dealing with menopause symptoms stole the show. Her comedic take on hormone creams and hot flashes added much-needed levity."*
- **Ebert:** *"Samantha’s subplot is the highlight, but the rest? Forgettable. Still, for her bold and unfiltered approach, I’ll give it a marginal thumbs up."*

### **5. Hot Flash Havoc (2016)**


<ul><li><strong>Siskel:</strong><br/><em>"This documentary is a bold and much-needed deep dive into the world of menopause, tackling the stigma and misinformation surrounding it. Hot Flash Havoc does an excellent job blending medical insights with personal stories, making it both informative and deeply relatable. I especially appreciated how it addressed the healthcare system’s failures, giving women the tools and knowledge they need to advocate for themselves. It’s not just a film—it’s a wake-up call."</em></li><li><strong>Ebert:</strong><br/><em>"You’re absolutely right, Gene. Hot Flash Havoc is not afraid to confront uncomfortable truths about how menopause is often dismissed or misunderstood. The expert interviews are compelling, especially when they break down the science of hormones and the risks of untreated symptoms. But what really struck me were the personal stories—women openly sharing their struggles and triumphs. It’s empowering and refreshingly honest. My only critique is that it occasionally leans too heavily on statistics, but it’s a minor quibble for such an important film. Two big thumbs up from me!"</em></li></ul>


### **6. Divine Secrets of the Ya-Ya Sisterhood** (2002)

- **Siskel:** *"While not directly about menopause, this film captures the emotional complexity of relationships during midlife. It’s more about generational connections, but the themes are deeply resonant."*
- **Ebert:** *"Agreed. The film shines in its portrayal of strong, flawed women. Menopause is in the background, but its impact is there. Thumbs up for the emotional depth."*

### **7. My Happy Ending** (2023)

- **Ebert:** *"Andie MacDowell delivers a stunning performance as a middle-aged woman grappling with menopause and health challenges. The blend of comedy and drama hits all the right notes."*
- **Siskel:** *"This one surprised me. It’s poignant, funny, and doesn’t shy away from the tough stuff. MacDowell shines, and so does the script. Two thumbs way up!"*

### **8. Calendar Girls** (2003)

- **Siskel:** *"While not explicitly about menopause, it’s a feel-good story about embracing life’s later stages with confidence. It’s charming and uplifting."*
- **Ebert:** *"A thoroughly enjoyable film. It’s more about aging gracefully than tackling menopause head-on, but it’s still worth a watch. Thumbs up for its humor and heart."*

### **9. Eat Pray Love** (2010)

- **Ebert:** *"This film doesn’t address menopause directly, but its themes of self-discovery and reinvention resonate deeply with women navigating midlife."*
- **Siskel:** *"A beautiful, introspective journey, though it’s more spiritual than biological. Still, its messages of empowerment and renewal are relevant. Thumbs up!"*

### Final Thoughts

- **Siskel:** *"Menopause is finally getting its moment in the spotlight, and these films show it can be funny, emotional, and even inspiring. It’s great to see stories that celebrate midlife and address the challenges women face."*
- **Ebert:** *"Agreed. Whether through documentaries, comedies, or heartfelt dramas, these movies help normalize conversations about menopause. Here’s to more stories that embrace this universal experience!"*
